Model Discrimination in the DES-SN5YR Hubble Diagram Is Highly Sensitive to the Simulation-Derived Bias-Correction Layer

T. McSheery · PhaseSpace Inc. · Draft 4.1, 20 August 2026 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22022089

Read the paper (PDF) Zenodo archive Code & data

Published Type Ia supernova distance moduli are not raw measurements — they carry a bias-correction layer computed from survey simulations generated under a reference cosmology. This paper measures how much leverage that layer has on model comparison in the public DES-SN5YR release, using two one-parameter distance laws and the full released covariance.

distance-modulus vectorΔχ² (probe − ΛCDM)
released MU (BBC-corrected)+1.1
reconstructed pre-BBC (MU + biasCor_mu)+12.8
pairwise discrimination leverage LBBC−11.7

The correction step moves the verdict by roughly eleven times the verdict's own magnitude — and moves it toward the alternative, so the concern is structural, not partisan. The sensitivity is not unique to the probe (flat wCDM: L = −5.7; Einstein–de Sitter control: L = +365, opposite sign). DES's own simulation reruns (Camilleri et al. 2024) indicate the reference-cosmology part of that leverage is small at this comparison's amplitude scale — the open question is precisely quantified for laws beyond it.

The paper makes no cosmological claim. It measures the size of the container, states what would settle the remainder, and invites falsification.

Addendum (2026-08-20): the −11.7 headline is specific to the superseded v1.2 release; on DES's recalibrated Dovekie release the same pre-registered measurement gives LBBC = −2.4 against a verdict of +1.8 — the concern was real for v1.2 and DES's own recalibration materially reduced it. Details in the table below and the consolidated addendum.
