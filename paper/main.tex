\begin{abstract}
Published Type Ia supernova distance moduli are not raw measurements. They
are the output of a processing chain that includes light-curve
standardization, Galactic-extinction treatment, photometric calibration,
redshift and peculiar-velocity corrections, and survey-bias corrections
derived from simulations. In the DES-SN5YR release, the BEAMS with Bias
Corrections (BBC) layer is obtained from simulations that assume a fiducial
cosmology together with intrinsic-scatter and population models.

We quantify the leverage of that correction step in the public DES-SN5YR
v1.2 Hubble-diagram release ($N = 1768$). The released BBC correction has
median $-0.05$~mag, range $-0.89$ to $+0.65$~mag, and a redshift regression
slope of $-0.125$~mag per unit $z$. Comparing flat $\Lambda$CDM against a
one-parameter non-expanding probe law with the full released covariance and
an analytically marginalized offset, the released vector gives
$\Delta\chi^2 \equiv \chi^2_B - \chi^2_A = +1.6$, weakly favouring
$\Lambda$CDM; the reconstructed pre-BBC vector
(\texttt{MU}${}+{}$\texttt{biasCor\_mu}) gives $+13.3$. Applying the
released BBC step therefore \emph{changes} the pairwise statistic by
$L_{\mathrm{BBC}} = -11.7$ $\chi^2$ units --- about seven times the final
discrimination --- and moves this comparison toward the probe rather than
toward the fiducial model, so the methodological concern raised here does
not arise from a correction that disadvantages the alternative.

Removing BBC simultaneously removes cosmology conditioning and restores
real survey selection ($\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ degrades from 0.93 to 1.34);
the reconstruction also retains the globally fitted standardization
parameters, making it a hybrid diagnostic rather than a full no-BBC
analysis. The 11.7-unit result therefore measures the \emph{total} leverage
of the BBC step; neither the magnitude nor the sign of the specifically
cosmology-conditioned component is isolated here. Propagating the twelve
BBC-input systematic modes released by DES bounds that component at
$\leq 0.7$ $\chi^2$ units in quadrature \emph{within the $\Lambda$CDM
neighbourhood}, confirming existing near-fiducial validation; no
corresponding bound exists for substantially different distance laws. For
the released 1.6-unit verdict merely to exceed the unknown conditioned
contribution, that contribution must be below $14\%$ of the measured total
swing. Second probes show the sensitivity is a property of wide-separation
comparisons rather than of the probe used to discover it: a fixed
Einstein--de Sitter control exhibits $|L_{\mathrm{BBC}}| \approx 367$ (with
opposite sign) and a flat $w$CDM probe ($w = -0.5$) gives
$|L_{\mathrm{BBC}}| = 5.0$. We define covariance-weighted projection and
fixed-shape/adaptive diagnostics, report their values, and propose paired
bias corrections against one empirically anchored selection model ---
reporting the verdict and its spread --- as the minimum requirement for
cosmology-independent adjudication. All inputs are public; a complete
reproduction recipe is given in \S\ref{sec:repro}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Type Ia supernovae provide one of the principal observational constraints
on the cosmic distance--redshift relation. Modern surveys do not compare
cosmological models directly with raw photon counts; they construct
standardized distance estimates through a processing chain incorporating
instrumental calibration, light-curve fitting, standardization relations,
redshift corrections, population assumptions, contamination treatment, and
corrections for survey selection. This distinction matters whenever the
statistical separation between competing distance laws is small.

The DES-SN5YR cosmological sample \citep{DES2024,Vincenzi2024,Sanchez2024}
is sufficiently precise that differences between plausible fitted models
can be of order a few units in $\chi^2$. At that scale a processing
component need not be large in absolute magnitude to affect model
discrimination; it need only project onto the difference between the
distance laws being compared.

This paper examines one such component: the BEAMS with Bias Corrections
framework \citep{KesslerScolnic2017} used in the DES-SN5YR analysis. BBC
uses survey simulations to estimate selection and fitting biases and
applies an event-level correction to the standardized distances. Those
simulations necessarily contain assumptions about the supernova
population, intrinsic scatter, survey selection, and a fiducial
distance--redshift relation.

The existence of this conditioning is not itself a defect. A flux-limited
survey genuinely requires selection modelling, and simulation is an
appropriate instrument for estimating effects that cannot be inferred from
detected events alone. Nor is input-cosmology dependence ignored in the
BBC literature: \citet{KesslerScolnic2017} explicitly tested variation of
the simulation cosmology within the conventional $w$CDM neighbourhood and
found the effect on recovered parameters to be small. That validation,
however, spans nearby cosmologies by construction. It has little to say
about comparisons against distance laws that are \emph{not} close to the
simulation cosmology --- precisely the comparisons for which the
conditioning is least benign, and the case measured here.

The question addressed is narrow and empirical: how much leverage does the
\emph{entire} BBC step have on a pairwise comparison against a
substantially different distance law, and is that leverage small or large
relative to the residual statistical discrimination? The experiment cannot
separate legitimate selection-bias correction from input-cosmology
conditioning; it measures the size and direction of the container in which
the unknown conditioned contribution resides. This paper proposes no
replacement cosmology. The alternative law is a probe, and readers may
substitute any smooth one-parameter alternative and repeat the exercise.

\section{Data and provenance}
\label{sec:data}

We use the public DES-SN5YR Hubble-diagram release, tag v1.2
\citep{DES2024,Sanchez2024}, corresponding to the original five-year
cosmology analysis rather than a later reanalysis. The relevant files are
\texttt{DES-SN5YR\_HD+MetaData.csv} (containing \texttt{zHD}, the released
modulus \texttt{MU}, its uncertainty \texttt{MUERR\_FINAL},
\texttt{biasCor\_mu}, SALT parameters \texttt{mB}, \texttt{x1}, \texttt{c},
survey identifiers, and peculiar-velocity information) and the released
systematic covariance. Per the release documentation the file
\texttt{STAT+SYS.txt.gz} contains systematics only; the statistical term is
carried in \texttt{MUERR\_FINAL} and the total covariance is
$C = C_{\mathrm{sys}} + \mathrm{diag}(\texttt{MUERR\_FINAL}^2)$.

Applying the release quality convention ($z_{\mathrm{HD}} \geq 0.01$,
$0 < \texttt{MUERR\_FINAL} \leq 10$) gives a fitting sample of $N = 1768$
spanning $0.025 \leq z \leq 1.121$: 1574 DES-discovered supernovae together
with 194 external low-redshift objects. The release version is frozen
because the DES-SN pipeline has subsequently evolved; all numbers here
refer to the v1.2 products.

\section{The processing layers}
\label{sec:layers}

\subsection{Framework-neutral or shared layers}

Photometric calibration and Galactic-extinction treatment are instrumental
or astrophysical corrections rather than assumptions about the cosmological
distance--redshift relation. The SALT light-curve fit
\citep{Kenworthy2021} also transforms the observed phase coordinate by the
measured time-dilation relation; DES light curves independently show an
approximately $(1+z)$ temporal broadening, $b = 1.003 \pm 0.005\,
(\mathrm{stat}) \pm 0.010\,(\mathrm{sys})$ \citep{White2024}. Any
framework tested against these standardized data must reproduce that
observation, so the present experiment treats the phase transformation as
shared processing.

\subsection{The BBC bias correction}
\label{sec:bbc}

The layer investigated directly is \texttt{biasCor\_mu}. DES documents the
released distance as
\begin{equation}
\mu = -2.5\log_{10} x_0 + \alpha x_1 - \beta c \pm \gamma/2
      - \mathrm{biasCor}_\mu - M_0 ,
\label{eq:mudef}
\end{equation}
with global $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ determined jointly with the
bias-corrected Hubble diagram. For the $N = 1768$ sample the released
correction has median $-0.05$~mag; range $-0.89$ to $+0.65$~mag; binned
medians, low to high $z$:
$(-0.053, -0.025, -0.069, -0.072, -0.139, -0.118)$~mag; and a linear
regression slope of $-0.125$~mag per unit $z$. The binned relation is not
monotonic; the regression slope is a compact description of the systematic
redshift dependence, not a claim that BBC follows a linear law. The
relevance of the correction is set not by amplitude but by its projection
onto the model difference, measured in \S\ref{sec:projection}.

\subsection{Peculiar velocities}

The 194 external low-redshift objects carry peculiar-velocity corrections
(median $|v| \approx 121$~km\,s$^{-1}$) from flow reconstructions
\citep{Peterson2022}. We do not treat these as evidence for the central BBC
claim. An anchor-exclusion variant is reported in \S\ref{sec:robust} as a
sample-composition sensitivity test only: removing those objects changes
redshift coverage and the normalization--shape degeneracy as well as the
correction treatment.

\section{Statistical question: discrimination leverage}
\label{sec:stat}

Let $\Delta\chi^2 \equiv \chi^2_B - \chi^2_A$, with Model~A flat
$\Lambda$CDM and Model~B the probe; positive values favour Model~A. For a
distance vector $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, model prediction $\boldsymbol{m}$, and
covariance $C$, we minimize
$\chi^2 = \boldsymbol{r}^{T} C^{-1} \boldsymbol{r}$ with
$\boldsymbol{r} = \boldsymbol{\mu} - \boldsymbol{m}$ after analytic
marginalization over an additive magnitude offset.

The pairwise BBC discrimination leverage is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}}
\equiv
\Delta\chi^2_{\mathrm{released}}
-
\Delta\chi^2_{\mathrm{preBBC}} .
\label{eq:lbbc}
\end{equation}
Negative $L_{\mathrm{BBC}}$ means that applying BBC shifts the comparison
toward Model~B relative to the reconstructed pre-BBC endpoint. The
statement applies to the total correction only; it does not determine the
sign of any cosmology-conditioned subcomponent.

Amplitude alone is not sufficient: a large correction can be irrelevant if
covariance-orthogonal to the model difference, and a small one decisive if
aligned. With the offset direction projected out (tildes), the
covariance-weighted cosine is
\begin{equation}
\rho_C =
\frac{
    \widetilde{\boldsymbol b}^{T} C^{-1} \widetilde{\boldsymbol d}
}{
    \sqrt{
        \bigl(\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}^{T} C^{-1}
              \widetilde{\boldsymbol b}\bigr)
        \bigl(\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}^{T} C^{-1}
              \widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\bigr)
    }
} ,
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{b}$ is the BBC vector and
$\boldsymbol{d} = \boldsymbol{m}_B - \boldsymbol{m}_A$ the model-separation
vector.

\section{The BBC bracket}
\label{sec:bracket}

\subsection{Empirical determination of the sign convention}
\label{sec:sign}

The sign of \texttt{biasCor\_mu} must be established before reconstructing
a pre-BBC vector. We determine it from the data: regressing
$(\mu_{\mathrm{target}} - m_B)$ on $(x_1, -c, 1)$ for the three candidate
conventions, only
$\texttt{MU} + \texttt{biasCor\_mu}$ reconstructs a Tripp relation
\citep{Tripp1998} with physically standard coefficients. The unconstrained
regression returns $\alpha = 0.15546 \pm 0.00038$ and
$\beta = 3.1159 \pm 0.0037$ with rms residual $0.0165$~mag --- near the DES
global values $\alpha = 0.16087$, $\beta = 3.11780$
\citep{Vincenzi2024}; the residual offset in $\alpha$ and the rms floor
reflect the omission of the host-mass step $\gamma$ and of per-event
weighting from this diagnostic regression. The released modulus therefore
contains the correction with a minus sign, per Eq.~\eqref{eq:mudef}, and
the reconstructed pre-BBC vector is
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{preBBC}} = \texttt{MU} + \texttt{biasCor\_mu} .
\label{eq:prebbc}
\end{equation}
An earlier draft of this analysis used the opposite convention, thereby
applying the correction twice, and reported a spurious verdict reversal;
the error was caught by this check before circulation.

\subsection{Distance laws}
\label{sec:laws}

\emph{Model A (flat $\Lambda$CDM).} The standard spatially flat luminosity
distance with one fitted shape parameter $\Omega_m$; the absolute
magnitude/$H_0$ combination is degenerate with the additive offset and is
analytically marginalized.

\emph{Model B (non-expanding phenomenological probe).}
\begin{equation}
D(z) = \frac{c}{K}\ln(1+z), \qquad
D_L(z) = D(z)\,(1+z),
\label{eq:modelB}
\end{equation}
with an attenuation term
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mu(z) = \frac{5}{\ln 10}\,\eta\,\tau(z), \qquad
\tau(z) = 1 - (1+z)^{-1/2},
\label{eq:atten}
\end{equation}
one fitted shape parameter $\eta$, and $K$ marginalized with the offset.
Model B supplies a smooth distance--redshift relation substantially
separated from the fiducial family; no physical claim is attached to it in
this paper.

There is no reversal of the pairwise ordering. Applying the released BBC
step \emph{changes} the statistic from $+13.3$ at the reconstructed
endpoint to $+1.6$ in the released vector:
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}} = 1.6 - 13.3 = -11.7 ,
\end{equation}
a leverage of about $7.3$ times the released discrimination, directed
toward Model~B. In this comparison the total correction moves the
statistic toward the alternative probe rather than toward the fiducial
model; the methodological concern raised here therefore does not arise
from objecting to a correction because it disadvantages Model~B. The
fitted shapes are strongly sensitive to the step
($\Omega_m: 0.50 \rightarrow 0.35$; $\eta: 0.05 \rightarrow 0.30$), which
establishes sensitivity, not that BBC ``supplies'' either model's
structure.

\subsection{What the bracket establishes, and what it does not}
\label{sec:limits}

The bracket establishes: (i) the BBC step has substantial
covariance-weighted leverage on this comparison; (ii) that leverage is
large relative to the statistic actually used to discriminate the models;
(iii) the direction of the \emph{total} correction is known. It does not
establish: that $\Lambda$CDM conditioning creates the released
near-degeneracy; the sign of the conditioned component; that the pre-BBC
vector is closer to the true Hubble diagram; that Model~B is physically
correct; or that every alternative law exhibits the same sensitivity
(tested in \S\ref{sec:robust}).

Three structural caveats govern the interpretation. First, removing the
correction restores genuine selection effects --- the degradation
$\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}: 0.93 \rightarrow 1.34$ is the tell that both models
are then fitting a $z$-dependent selection ramp neither describes ---
and the released covariance is not the covariance of the reconstructed
vector, so only differences within a row of Table~\ref{tab:headline} are
quoted. Second, the reconstructed pre-BBC vector is a \emph{hybrid
diagnostic} rather than the result of rerunning the analysis without BBC:
adding \texttt{biasCor\_mu} removes the released event-level magnitude
correction but leaves in place the globally determined standardization
parameters $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, together with other quantities
obtained within the nominal analysis. A complete pre-BBC reconstruction
would require refitting those nuisance parameters jointly with the altered
selection treatment; the direction and magnitude of this residual
nuisance-parameter conditioning are not determined by the present bracket.
Third, writing the measured total schematically as
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}} =
L_{\mathrm{selection}} + L_{\mathrm{population}} + L_{\mathrm{scatter}}
+ L_{\mathrm{classification}} + L_{\mathrm{cosmology}}
+ L_{\mathrm{interactions}} ,
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
the experiment measures the left-hand side only.

\subsection{How small must the conditioned component be?}
\label{sec:bound}

The released comparison is decided by $\Delta\chi^2 = 1.6$; the total
correction-step swing is $11.7$. For the unknown cosmology-conditioned
contribution merely to remain smaller than the nominal discrimination
requires $|L_{\mathrm{cosmology}}| < 1.6$, i.e.\ less than
$1.6/11.7 \approx 14\%$ of the observed total swing --- and considerably
smaller to be negligible. Existing near-fiducial validation does not
establish that the cosmology-conditioned contribution satisfies this bound
for substantially separated distance laws.

Three observations. First, the leverage exceeds the released verdict in
every variant. Second, the sensitivity is a property of wide-separation
comparisons rather than of the discovering probe: the Einstein--de Sitter
control --- a completely conventional non-accelerating FLRW model with no
free shape parameter --- exhibits leverage thirty times the headline
value, and even a moderate-separation $w$CDM probe carries three times the
released verdict. The Einstein--de Sitter case is a sensitivity control
only (its absolute $\chi^2$ is catastrophic on either vector, as
expected); what matters is $|L_{\mathrm{BBC}}| \approx 367$. Third, the
\emph{sign} of the leverage varies by probe ($+367$, $-11.7$, $-5.0$): the
correction moved the Einstein--de Sitter comparison in $\Lambda$CDM's
favour and the Model-B comparison toward Model~B. The layer displays no
consistent partisanship; its direction is set by the geometry of each
comparison.

The statistical-only variant demonstrates that covariance treatment
changes the numerical leverage materially and cannot replace the full
covariance in the headline. The anchor-exclusion variant is a
sample-composition sensitivity test (\S\ref{sec:layers}); the released
near-degeneracy is sensitive to inclusion of the low-redshift anchors, but
attribution to the peculiar-velocity layer specifically is not established
by it.

\subsection{Covariance-weighted projection}
\label{sec:projection}

Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho} with $\boldsymbol{d}$ at the released-vector
best fits ($\Omega_m = 0.35$, $\eta = 0.30$):
\begin{equation}
\rho_C = -0.065, \qquad
\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}\rVert_{C^{-1}} = 29.76, \qquad
\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\rVert_{C^{-1}} = 2.51 .
\label{eq:rhovals}
\end{equation}
Repeating with $\boldsymbol{d}$ at the pre-BBC best fits
($\Omega_m = 0.50$, $\eta = 0.05$) gives $\rho_C = -0.086$: the diagnostic
is stable against the choice of evaluation point.

The geometry is the central quantitative fact of this paper. The BBC
vector is approximately $94\%$ covariance-orthogonal to the model
difference --- as an honest selection correction should be; it is not
``aimed'' at the comparison --- yet its norm in the covariance metric is
so large that the residual $6.5\%$ projection alone carries several times
the released verdict. For the verdict to be robust, the
\emph{conditioned part} of the correction must satisfy
$2\,\lvert\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}_{\mathrm{cond}}^{T} C^{-1}
\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\rvert < 1.6$ --- an effective misalignment below
about one percent. That is a precision statement about a simulation
output which existing validation was never designed to certify.

\subsection{Fixed-shape versus adaptive leverage}
\label{sec:fixed}

Holding both models' shape parameters at the released-vector best fits and
recomputing the bracket separates the direct effect of the correction from
parameter accommodation:
\begin{align}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}}^{\mathrm{fixed}}
&= 2\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}^{T} C^{-1} \widetilde{\boldsymbol d}
 = 2\rho_C\,
   \lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}\rVert_{C^{-1}}
   \lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\rVert_{C^{-1}}
 \nonumber\\
&\simeq 2\,(-0.065)(29.76)(2.51) = -9.7 ,
\label{eq:fixed}
\end{align}
verified independently by direct evaluation of the fixed-shape bracket
($-9.72$). The remainder,
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}}^{\mathrm{adapt}}
= L_{\mathrm{BBC}} - L_{\mathrm{BBC}}^{\mathrm{fixed}}
= -2.0 ,
\label{eq:adapt}
\end{equation}
is the contribution of parameter accommodation. Most of the leverage is
direct alignment of the correction with the model difference, and the
agreement between the projection identity and the independently evaluated
fixed-shape bracket is an internal consistency check on the whole
computation.

\section{Isolating the cosmology-conditioned component}
\label{sec:isolate}

\subsection{The DES systematic subspace: computed}
\label{sec:syssub}

The release provides single-systematic covariance blocks for the
correction layer's simulation inputs: the intrinsic-scatter model (BS20),
dust/population realizations (P21SYS1--3, P21\_HOSTCOLOR, W22\_AGE), host
library (SVAHOSTLIB), selection efficiency (HOSTEFFshift), core-collapse
likelihood (CClikelihood), and classifier swaps (SNNtraining, SNIRF,
SCONE). Each block is (near) rank-1, $C_k \approx
\boldsymbol{d}_k \boldsymbol{d}_k^{T}$, the covariance of a single input
swap. Perturbing the released moduli by $\pm\boldsymbol{d}_k$ and
refitting both models yields per-input verdict shifts between $\pm 0.01$
and $\pm 0.40$ $\chi^2$ units, with quadrature sum
\begin{equation}
\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_k \max\bigl(|\delta\Delta\chi^2_k|\bigr)^2
\Bigr)^{1/2} = 0.71
\label{eq:quad}
\end{equation}
against the released margin of $1.6$. Within DES's own declared error
budget, then, the conditioned component does not overturn the verdict ---
the near-fiducial validation of \citet{KesslerScolnic2017} is confirmed on
this dataset by an independent route. The caveat is the one that motivates
this paper: every one of these variations is computed inside $\Lambda$CDM
simulations. They measure input sensitivity \emph{within the
neighbourhood}, not the conditioning against a distant distance law. The
finding is therefore three-tiered: total step leverage $\approx 12$
(\S\ref{sec:headline}); within-neighbourhood conditioned component
$\leq 0.7$ (this section); conditioning against distant laws --- bounded
between those figures --- measured by no one.

\subsection{Rerunning BBC under distant input laws}

The direct test regenerates the correction with the simulation's input
distance--redshift relation changed, all other architecture held fixed.
With correction vectors $\boldsymbol{b}_A$ (fiducial input) and
$\boldsymbol{b}_B$ (distant-alternative input), the discriminating
quantity is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{cosmology}} =
\Delta\chi^2(\boldsymbol{b}_B) - \Delta\chi^2(\boldsymbol{b}_A) ,
\label{eq:lcosmo}
\end{equation}
to be compared with $1.6$. If it is of order $0.05$ the concern raised
here is largely resolved; if of order $2$ the nominal discrimination is
not conditioning-independent. Both outcomes are scientifically useful; the
paper asks for the measurement, not a presumption.

\subsection{Paired corrections, and the definitive instrument}

A useful intermediate is a paired-correction experiment: one common,
empirically anchored survey-selection model; corrections computed under
each candidate law; the verdict reported together with its spread
$S_{\mathrm{conditioning}} = |\Delta\chi^2_A - \Delta\chi^2_B|$ across the
two schemes. If the spread is comparable to the claimed discrimination,
corrected-modulus comparison has not produced a conditioning-independent
verdict. The definitive instrument is likelihood-level forward modelling:
each candidate law propagated through the same population,
intrinsic-scatter, cadence, detection, classification, and cut
architecture, compared with the survey at the likelihood level. Selection
is a generative problem; simulation should not be eliminated but shared.

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

The analysis requires the public v1.2 release and a scientific Python
environment; the reference implementation is at
\href{https://github.com/tracyphasespace/Model-Discrimination-DES-SN5YR}{github.com/tracyphasespace/Model-Discrimination-DES-SN5YR}
(six scripts; each regenerates the numbers of the section named).

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Freeze the data.} Fetch \texttt{DES-SN5YR\_HD+MetaData.csv}
  and \texttt{STAT+SYS.txt.gz} at tag v1.2; record hashes
  (\texttt{data/fetch.sh}).
\item \emph{Load and clean.} Apply the release quality convention; confirm
  $N = 1768$ and the covariance construction
  $C = C_{\mathrm{sys}} + \mathrm{diag}(\texttt{MUERR\_FINAL}^2)$
  (positive-definite; $C_{\mathrm{sys}}$ alone is not).
\item \emph{Establish the sign empirically} (\texttt{sign\_check.py};
  \S\ref{sec:sign}). Do not proceed until this passes.
\item \emph{Characterize the layer} without reference to Model B: median,
  extrema, binned medians, regression slope.
\item \emph{Implement both laws} exactly as \S\ref{sec:laws}; marginalize
  the offset analytically; never fit $H_0$, $K$, or $M$ alongside.
\item \emph{Fit the released vector} (\texttt{bracket.py}); confirm
  $\Delta\chi^2 = +1.6$.
\item \emph{Reconstruct} $\mu_{\mathrm{preBBC}}$ per
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:prebbc}; refit; confirm $+13.3$; report
  $L_{\mathrm{BBC}}$.
\item \emph{Projection and decomposition} (\texttt{projection.py});
  confirm Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rhovals}--\eqref{eq:adapt}, including the
  identity check.
\item \emph{Robustness and probes} (\texttt{robustness.py},
  \texttt{probes.py}); confirm Table~\ref{tab:probes}.
\item \emph{Systematic subspace} (\texttt{isolate\_conditioning.py};
  requires \texttt{SingleSYS\_CovMatrix/}); confirm
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:quad}.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Falsification criterion.} The central claim fails if an independent
implementation with the verified sign convention, frozen v1.2 data,
matched covariance ordering, full covariance, and correct offset
marginalization produces a correction-step swing small relative to the
released discrimination. We invite that check specifically. The first
major implementation error found by this procedure was the authors' own
sign error (\S\ref{sec:sign}); that is why the procedure is included.

\section{Discussion}

The result should not be read as an indictment of simulation-based bias
correction. A flux-limited survey requires a model of the objects it fails
to observe, and the pre-BBC endpoint demonstrates the point as clearly as
it demonstrates sensitivity: the goodness of fit deteriorates
substantially when the correction is backed out. The methodological issue
arises only when a corrected data product is used to distinguish models
whose distance--redshift relations are far from the one used in the
simulations. Two questions must not be conflated: whether BBC successfully
corrects survey bias when the assumed and tested cosmologies are near one
another (existing validation, plus \S\ref{sec:syssub}, support this), and
whether the corrected Hubble diagram is effectively neutral when
discriminating against a substantially different law (untested). For the
pair examined here the total step carries $11.7$ units of leverage against
a $1.6$-unit verdict; a possible conditioning effect lives inside a
transformation whose leverage is much larger than the verdict, with a
residual projection requirement at the one-percent level
(\S\ref{sec:projection}). The paper asks for a measurement, not a
presumption.

\section{Conclusion}

The DES-SN5YR v1.2 release carries a simulation-derived BBC correction
with substantial redshift structure. For the probes examined the released
vector gives $\Delta\chi^2 = +1.6$ (Model B), while the reconstructed
pre-BBC vector gives $+13.3$; the pairwise discrimination leverage is
$L_{\mathrm{BBC}} = -11.7$, about $7.3$ times the released
discrimination, directed toward the probe. Second probes establish the
pattern as a property of wide-separation comparisons ---
Einstein--de Sitter, $|L| \approx 367$ with opposite sign; $w$CDM
($w = -0.5$), $|L| = 5.0$ --- with probe-dependent sign and hence no
consistent partisanship. The BBC vector is $94\%$ covariance-orthogonal to
the model difference, yet its residual projection alone carries six times
the verdict; most of the leverage is direct alignment
($L^{\mathrm{fixed}} = -9.7$) rather than parameter accommodation
($L^{\mathrm{adapt}} = -2.0$). DES's own single-systematic modes bound the
conditioned component at $\leq 0.7$ units within the $\Lambda$CDM
neighbourhood; no bound exists for distant laws, and for the released
verdict to be meaningful the conditioned contribution must be below $14\%$
of the measured swing. That requirement should be tested rather than
assumed --- by systematic-mode propagation (done here), by matched reruns
of the simulation chain under distant input laws, and ultimately by
likelihood-level forward modelling through a shared survey architecture.
Until then, few-$\Delta\chi^2$ discrimination between a fiducial family
and a substantially different distance law should be interpreted as
conditional on the demonstrated neutrality of the simulation-derived
correction layer, not assumed to possess that neutrality by construction.

\section*{Author's note on scope}

This paper contains no proposed cosmological framework. The law of
\S\ref{sec:laws} is a probe used to test the sensitivity of pairwise model
discrimination to a simulation-derived correction layer. A reader may
reject that law physically without affecting the empirical
characterization of \texttt{biasCor\_mu}, the reconstruction of the
pre-BBC vector, or the methodological requirement that the
cosmology-conditioned component be measured when the correction step has
leverage substantially larger than the verdict. A separate treatment of
the propagation physics motivating the functional form of Model B is
logically independent of this analysis.
