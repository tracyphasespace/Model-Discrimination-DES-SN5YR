\begin{abstract}
Published Type Ia supernova distance moduli are not raw measurements. They
are the output of a processing chain that includes light-curve
standardization, Galactic-extinction treatment, photometric calibration,
redshift and peculiar-velocity corrections, and survey-bias corrections
derived from simulations. In the DES-SN5YR release, the BEAMS with Bias
Corrections (BBC) layer is obtained from simulations generated under a
\emph{reference} cosmology together with intrinsic-scatter and population
models.

We quantify the leverage of that correction step in the public DES-SN5YR
v1.2 Hubble-diagram release, using the official likelihood's redshift
convention ($z_{\mathrm{HEL}}$ in the external luminosity-distance factor,
$z_{\mathrm{HD}}$ in the cosmological integral). The released BBC
correction has median $-0.05$~mag, range $-0.89$ to $+0.65$~mag, and a
redshift regression slope of $-0.125$~mag per unit $z$. Comparing flat
$\Lambda$CDM against a one-parameter non-expanding probe law with the full
released covariance and an analytically marginalized offset, the released
vector gives $\Delta\chi^2 \equiv \chi^2_B - \chi^2_A = +1.1$, weakly
favouring $\Lambda$CDM; the reconstructed pre-BBC vector
(\texttt{MU}${}+{}$\texttt{biasCor\_mu}) gives $+12.8$. Applying the
released BBC step therefore \emph{changes} the pairwise statistic by
$L_{\mathrm{BBC}} = -11.7$ $\chi^2$ units --- roughly eleven times the
final discrimination --- and moves this comparison toward Model B rather than
toward the flat-$\Lambda$CDM comparator, so the concern raised here does
not arise from a correction that disadvantages the alternative. The result is
unchanged when all 1829 released entries are retained.

The bracket measures the \emph{total} leverage of the BBC step: removing
the correction simultaneously restores real survey selection
($\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ degrades from 0.93 to 1.34), and the reconstruction
retains the globally fitted standardization parameters, making it a hybrid
diagnostic. Neither the magnitude nor the sign of any specifically
reference-cosmology-conditioned component is isolated by the bracket.
Crucially, DES has already tested that component directly:
\citet{Camilleri2024} rerun the bias-correction simulations under
reference cosmologies spanning distance-modulus variations of
$\sim 0.15$~mag and find the effect subdominant to statistical
uncertainties. We compute that the released-best-fit probe law differs in
offset-removed shape from $\Lambda$CDM by at most $0.055$~mag over the
sample --- well below that tested amplitude (an amplitude comparison;
Camilleri's formal criterion is the shape envelope bounded by their
Fig.~4e reference curves, and the pointwise containment test against
those boundary cosmologies is the direct completion). The finding is
therefore sharper than a missing-validation claim: \emph{the total BBC
layer carries pairwise leverage an order of magnitude larger than the
verdict even though prior DES simulation tests indicate the
reference-cosmology part of that leverage is small at the amplitude scale
of this comparison.} The untested regime begins at larger departures ---
our Einstein--de Sitter control ($0.276$~mag, $1.8\times$ the tested
amplitude) exhibits $|L_{\mathrm{BBC}}| \approx 365$ --- and for
functional forms outside the FLRW family the tested coverage is an
inference to be completed, not assumed. We report covariance-weighted
projection and fixed/adaptive diagnostics, the verdict sensitivity to
twelve released BBC-related systematic modes ($0.71$ in quadrature), and
propose matched-simulation reruns and paired corrections as the
completion of the validation programme. All inputs are public; a complete
reproduction recipe is given in \S\ref{sec:repro}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Type Ia supernovae provide one of the principal observational constraints
on the cosmic distance--redshift relation. Modern surveys do not compare
cosmological models directly with raw photon counts; they construct
standardized distance estimates through a processing chain incorporating
instrumental calibration, light-curve fitting, standardization relations,
redshift corrections, population assumptions, contamination treatment, and
corrections for survey selection. This distinction matters whenever the
statistical separation between competing distance laws is small.

The DES-SN5YR cosmological sample \citep{DES2024,Vincenzi2024,Sanchez2024}
is sufficiently precise that differences between plausible fitted models
can be of order a few units in $\chi^2$. At that scale a processing
component need not be large in absolute magnitude to affect model
discrimination; it need only project onto the difference between the
distance laws being compared.

This paper examines one such component: the BEAMS with Bias Corrections
framework \citep{KesslerScolnic2017} used in the DES-SN5YR analysis. BBC
uses survey simulations to estimate selection and fitting biases and
applies an event-level correction to the standardized distances. Two
distinct cosmology inputs appear in that machinery and must not be
conflated \citep{Camilleri2024}: the \emph{reference} cosmology used to
generate the bias-correction simulations, and the \emph{fiducial}
cosmology used internally while determining the standardization nuisance
parameters and redshift-bin offsets. Throughout this paper
``conditioning'' refers to dependence on the reference cosmology unless
stated otherwise.

The existence of this conditioning is not itself a defect, and it is not
untested. \citet{KesslerScolnic2017} varied the simulation cosmology
within the conventional $w$CDM neighbourhood and found small effects on
recovered parameters. More directly, \citet{Camilleri2024} rerun the
DES-SN5YR bias-correction simulations under reference cosmologies
differing from the baseline by up to $\sim 10\sigma$ --- distance-modulus
variations of roughly $0.15$~mag --- including nonstandard $w_0 w_a$
truth models, and find the impact subdominant to statistical
uncertainties, providing an iterative prescription for cases beyond. The
open questions are therefore quantitative and specific: how large is the
\emph{total} BBC step's leverage on a pairwise comparison; where does a
given alternative law sit relative to the validated reference-cosmology
envelope; and what remains for laws outside it or outside the FLRW
functional family. Those are the questions measured here. This paper
proposes no replacement cosmology; the alternative law is a probe, and
readers may substitute any smooth one-parameter alternative.

\section{Data and provenance}
\label{sec:data}

We use the public DES-SN5YR Hubble-diagram release, tag v1.2
\citep{DES2024,Sanchez2024}. The relevant files are
\texttt{DES-SN5YR\_HD+MetaData.csv} (containing $z_{\mathrm{HD}}$,
$z_{\mathrm{HEL}}$, the released modulus \texttt{MU}, its uncertainty
\texttt{MUERR\_FINAL}, \texttt{biasCor\_mu}, SALT parameters \texttt{mB},
\texttt{x1}, \texttt{c}, survey identifiers, and peculiar-velocity
information) and the released systematic covariance. Per the release
documentation, \texttt{STAT+SYS.txt.gz} contains systematics only; the
statistical term is carried in \texttt{MUERR\_FINAL} and the total
covariance is $C = C_{\mathrm{sys}} +
\mathrm{diag}(\texttt{MUERR\_FINAL}^2)$.

The v1.2 release contains 1829 Hubble-diagram entries. Our default sample
excludes 61 effectively zero-weight entries with
$\texttt{MUERR\_FINAL} > 10$, leaving $N = 1768$ over
$0.025 \leq z_{\mathrm{HD}} \leq 1.121$ (1574 DES-discovered supernovae
plus 194 external low-redshift objects); this is our diagnostic cut, not
the official likelihood convention, which retains all 1829. As a
robustness check, retaining all 1829 entries changes no reported number at
the quoted precision. The release version is frozen because the DES-SN
pipeline has subsequently evolved.

Distance moduli are compared with model predictions using the official
likelihood's redshift convention: the cosmological integral is evaluated
at $z_{\mathrm{HD}}$ while the external luminosity-distance factor uses
the heliocentric redshift, $\mu \propto
5\log_{10}[(1+z_{\mathrm{HEL}})\,D_M(z_{\mathrm{HD}})]$. The probe law
adopts the analogous deliberate assignment (\S\ref{sec:laws}).

\section{The processing layers}
\label{sec:layers}

\subsection{Framework-neutral or shared layers}

Photometric calibration and Galactic-extinction treatment are instrumental
or astrophysical corrections rather than assumptions about the
cosmological distance--redshift relation. The SALT light-curve fit
\citep{Kenworthy2021} also transforms the observed phase coordinate by the
measured time-dilation relation; DES light curves independently show an
approximately $(1+z)$ temporal broadening, $b = 1.003 \pm 0.005\,
(\mathrm{stat}) \pm 0.010\,(\mathrm{sys})$ \citep{White2024}. Any
framework tested against these standardized data must reproduce that
observation, so the present experiment treats the phase transformation as
shared processing.

\subsection{The BBC bias correction}
\label{sec:bbc}

The layer investigated directly is \texttt{biasCor\_mu}. DES documents the
released distance as
\begin{equation}
\mu = -2.5\log_{10} x_0 + \alpha x_1 - \beta c \pm \gamma/2
      - \mathrm{biasCor}_\mu - M_0 ,
\label{eq:mudef}
\end{equation}
with global $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ determined jointly with the
bias-corrected Hubble diagram. For the $N = 1768$ sample the released
correction has median $-0.05$~mag; range $-0.89$ to $+0.65$~mag; binned
medians, low to high $z$:
$(-0.053, -0.025, -0.069, -0.072, -0.139, -0.118)$~mag; and a linear
regression slope of $-0.125$~mag per unit $z$. The binned relation is not
monotonic; the regression slope is a compact description of the systematic
redshift dependence, not a claim that BBC follows a linear law. The
relevance of the correction is set not by amplitude but by its projection
onto the model difference, measured in \S\ref{sec:projection}.

\subsection{Peculiar velocities}

The 194 external low-redshift objects carry peculiar-velocity corrections
(median $|v| \approx 121$~km\,s$^{-1}$) from flow reconstructions
\citep{Peterson2022}. We do not treat these as evidence for the central
BBC claim. An anchor-exclusion variant is reported in \S\ref{sec:robust}
as a sample-composition sensitivity test only.

\section{Statistical question: discrimination leverage}
\label{sec:stat}

Let $\Delta\chi^2 \equiv \chi^2_B - \chi^2_A$, with Model~A flat
$\Lambda$CDM and Model~B the probe; positive values favour Model~A. For a
distance vector $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, model prediction $\boldsymbol{m}$, and
covariance $C$, we minimize
$\chi^2 = \boldsymbol{r}^{T} C^{-1} \boldsymbol{r}$ with
$\boldsymbol{r} = \boldsymbol{\mu} - \boldsymbol{m}$ after analytic
marginalization over an additive magnitude offset.

The pairwise BBC discrimination leverage is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}}
\equiv
\Delta\chi^2_{\mathrm{released}}
-
\Delta\chi^2_{\mathrm{preBBC}} .
\label{eq:lbbc}
\end{equation}
Negative $L_{\mathrm{BBC}}$ means that applying BBC shifts the comparison
toward Model~B relative to the reconstructed pre-BBC endpoint. The
statement applies to the total correction only.

Amplitude alone is not sufficient: a large correction can be irrelevant if
covariance-orthogonal to the model difference, and a small one decisive if
aligned. With the offset direction projected out (tildes), the
covariance-weighted cosine is
\begin{equation}
\rho_C =
\frac{
    \widetilde{\boldsymbol b}^{T} C^{-1} \widetilde{\boldsymbol d}
}{
    \sqrt{
        \bigl(\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}^{T} C^{-1}
              \widetilde{\boldsymbol b}\bigr)
        \bigl(\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}^{T} C^{-1}
              \widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\bigr)
    }
} ,
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{b}$ is the BBC vector and
$\boldsymbol{d} = \boldsymbol{m}_B - \boldsymbol{m}_A$ the model-separation
vector.

\section{The BBC bracket}
\label{sec:bracket}

\subsection{Empirical determination of the sign convention}
\label{sec:sign}

The sign of \texttt{biasCor\_mu} must be established before reconstructing
a pre-BBC vector. We determine it from the data: regressing
$(\mu_{\mathrm{target}} - m_B)$ on $(x_1, -c, 1)$ for the three candidate
conventions, only $\texttt{MU} + \texttt{biasCor\_mu}$ reconstructs a
Tripp relation \citep{Tripp1998} with physically standard coefficients.
The unconstrained regression returns $\alpha = 0.15546 \pm 0.00038$ and
$\beta = 3.1159 \pm 0.0037$ with rms residual $0.0165$~mag --- near the
DES global values $\alpha = 0.16087$, $\beta = 3.11780$
\citep{Vincenzi2024}; the residual offset in $\alpha$ and the rms floor
reflect the omission of the host-mass step $\gamma$ and of per-event
weighting from this diagnostic regression. The released modulus therefore
contains the correction with a minus sign, per Eq.~\eqref{eq:mudef}, and
the reconstructed pre-BBC vector is
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{preBBC}} = \texttt{MU} + \texttt{biasCor\_mu} .
\label{eq:prebbc}
\end{equation}
An earlier draft of this analysis used the opposite convention, thereby
applying the correction twice, and reported a spurious verdict reversal;
the error was caught by this check before circulation.

\subsection{Distance laws}
\label{sec:laws}

\emph{Model A (flat $\Lambda$CDM).} The standard spatially flat luminosity
distance evaluated per the official convention,
$\mu_A \propto 5\log_{10}[(1+z_{\mathrm{HEL}})\,D_M(z_{\mathrm{HD}})]$,
with one fitted shape parameter $\Omega_m$; the absolute magnitude/$H_0$
combination is degenerate with the additive offset and is analytically
marginalized.

\emph{Model B (non-expanding phenomenological probe).}
\begin{equation}
D(z_{\mathrm{HD}}) = \frac{c}{K}\ln(1+z_{\mathrm{HD}}), \qquad
\mu_B \propto 5\log_{10}\!\bigl[(1+z_{\mathrm{HEL}})\,
D(z_{\mathrm{HD}})\bigr] + \Delta\mu ,
\label{eq:modelB}
\end{equation}
with attenuation
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mu(z_{\mathrm{HD}})
= \frac{5}{\ln 10}\,\eta\,\tau(z_{\mathrm{HD}}), \qquad
\tau(z) = 1 - (1+z)^{-1/2},
\label{eq:atten}
\end{equation}
one fitted shape parameter $\eta$, and $K$ marginalized with the offset.
The redshift assignment is deliberate: observed-frame flux factors carry
$z_{\mathrm{HEL}}$; the accumulated path and attenuation coordinate is
$z_{\mathrm{HD}}$. Model B supplies a smooth distance--redshift relation substantially
different in functional form from the baseline FLRW relation; no physical
claim is attached to it here.

There is no reversal of the pairwise ordering. Applying the released BBC
step \emph{changes} the statistic from $+12.8$ at the reconstructed
endpoint to $+1.1$ in the released vector:
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}} = 1.1 - 12.8 = -11.7 ,
\end{equation}
a leverage of roughly eleven times the released discrimination, directed
toward Model~B. In this comparison the total correction moves the
statistic toward the alternative probe rather than toward the
flat-$\Lambda$CDM comparator; the methodological concern raised here
therefore does not arise
from objecting to a correction because it disadvantages Model~B. The
fitted shapes are strongly sensitive to the step
($\Omega_m: 0.499 \rightarrow 0.352$; $\eta: 0.049 \rightarrow 0.297$),
which establishes sensitivity, not that BBC ``supplies'' either model's
structure.

\subsection{What the bracket establishes, and what it does not}
\label{sec:limits}

The bracket establishes: (i) the BBC step has substantial
covariance-weighted leverage on this comparison; (ii) that leverage is an
order of magnitude larger than the statistic used to discriminate the
models; (iii) the direction of the \emph{total} correction is known. It
does not establish: that reference-cosmology conditioning creates the
released near-degeneracy; the sign of the conditioned component; that the
pre-BBC vector is closer to the true Hubble diagram; that Model~B is
physically correct; or that every alternative law exhibits the same
sensitivity (tested in \S\ref{sec:robust}).

Three structural caveats govern the interpretation. First, removing the
correction restores genuine selection effects --- the degradation
$\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}: 0.93 \rightarrow 1.34$ is the tell that both models
are then fitting a $z$-dependent selection ramp neither describes --- and
the released covariance is not the covariance of the reconstructed vector,
so only differences within a row of Table~\ref{tab:headline} are quoted.
Second, the reconstructed pre-BBC vector is a \emph{hybrid diagnostic}
rather than the result of rerunning the analysis without BBC: adding
\texttt{biasCor\_mu} removes the released event-level magnitude correction
but leaves in place the globally determined standardization parameters
$\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, together with other quantities obtained
within the nominal analysis; the direction and magnitude of this residual
nuisance-parameter conditioning are not determined by the present bracket.
Third, writing the measured total schematically as
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}} =
L_{\mathrm{selection}} + L_{\mathrm{population}} + L_{\mathrm{scatter}}
+ L_{\mathrm{classification}} + L_{\mathrm{cosmology}}
+ L_{\mathrm{interactions}} ,
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
the experiment measures the left-hand side only; the components can add,
cancel, and interact, so no term is individually bounded by the total.

\subsection{How small must the conditioned component be?}
\label{sec:bound}

The released comparison is decided by $\Delta\chi^2 = 1.1$; the total
correction-step swing is $11.7$. For the reference-cosmology contribution
merely to remain smaller than the nominal discrimination requires
$|L_{\mathrm{cosmology}}| < 1.1$, i.e.\ below roughly $9\%$ of the
observed total swing --- and considerably smaller to be negligible.
\S\ref{sec:envelope} addresses how much of that requirement prior DES
validation already discharges for this probe.

BBC leverage is substantial across the tested comparisons; it exceeds the
released discrimination in the headline Model-B comparison, the
$w = -0.5$ probe, and the statistical-only variant, while for the
Einstein--de Sitter control and the anchor-excluded variant the leverage,
though large in absolute terms, is smaller than those comparisons' own
(much larger) released margins. The sensitivity is not unique to Model B:
substantial leverage is also observed for two additional separated
distance-law probes. The \emph{sign} of the leverage varies by probe
($+365$, $-11.7$, $-5.7$): the correction moved the Einstein--de Sitter
comparison in $\Lambda$CDM's favour and the Model-B comparison toward
Model~B, so the layer displays no consistent partisanship; its direction
is set by the geometry of each comparison. The Einstein--de Sitter case
is a sensitivity control only (its absolute $\chi^2$ is catastrophic on
either vector, as expected). The statistical-only variant shows that
covariance treatment changes the numerical leverage materially and cannot
replace the full covariance. The anchor-exclusion variant is a
sample-composition test: it removes redshift coverage and the
normalization--shape lever arm along with the peculiar-velocity layer, so
no attribution to the latter is made.

\subsection{Covariance-weighted projection}
\label{sec:projection}

Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho} with $\boldsymbol{d}$ at the tabulated
three-decimal released-vector best-fit values
($\Omega_m = 0.352$, $\eta = 0.297$; evaluating instead at full-precision
optimizer outputs shifts $L^{\mathrm{fixed}}$ by $\sim 0.2$):
\begin{equation}
\rho_C = -0.068, \qquad
\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}\rVert_{C^{-1}} = 29.76, \qquad
\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\rVert_{C^{-1}} = 2.51 .
\label{eq:rhovals}
\end{equation}
Repeating with $\boldsymbol{d}$ at the pre-BBC best fits gives
$\rho_C = -0.077$: the diagnostic is stable against the evaluation point.

The covariance-weighted cosine is small --- the vectors are nearly
covariance-orthogonal, as an honest selection correction should be; the
layer is not ``aimed'' at the comparison --- yet the BBC vector's norm in
the covariance metric is large enough that its residual projection alone
dominates the released margin (\S\ref{sec:fixed}). The general
requirement on the conditioned component follows from
$2\,\lvert\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}_{\mathrm{cond}}^{T} C^{-1}
\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\rvert < 1.1$:
\begin{equation}
\lvert\rho_{\mathrm{cond}}\rvert \,
\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}_{\mathrm{cond}}\rVert_{C^{-1}}
< \frac{1.1}{2\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\rVert_{C^{-1}}}
\simeq 0.22 ,
\label{eq:condreq}
\end{equation}
a joint bound on the conditioned component's norm and alignment; no
assumption about its norm is made.

\subsection{Fixed-shape versus adaptive leverage}
\label{sec:fixed}

Holding both models' shape parameters at the released-vector best fits and
recomputing the bracket separates the direct effect of the correction from
parameter accommodation:
\begin{align}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}}^{\mathrm{fixed}}
&= 2\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}^{T} C^{-1} \widetilde{\boldsymbol d}
 = 2\rho_C\,
   \lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol b}\rVert_{C^{-1}}
   \lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol d}\rVert_{C^{-1}}
 \nonumber\\
&\simeq 2\,(-0.068)(29.76)(2.51) = -10.1 ,
\label{eq:fixed}
\end{align}
verified independently by direct evaluation of the fixed-shape bracket.
The remainder,
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{BBC}}^{\mathrm{adapt}}
= L_{\mathrm{BBC}} - L_{\mathrm{BBC}}^{\mathrm{fixed}}
= -1.6 ,
\label{eq:adapt}
\end{equation}
is the contribution of parameter accommodation. Most of the leverage is
direct alignment of the correction with the model difference, and the
agreement between the projection identity and the independently evaluated
fixed-shape bracket is an internal consistency check on the computation.

\section{The reference-cosmology component}
\label{sec:isolate}

\subsection{Where the probe sits relative to the tested amplitude scale}
\label{sec:envelope}

\citet{Camilleri2024} distinguish the reference cosmology (generating the
bias-correction simulations) from the fiducial cosmology (used inside the
BBC nuisance fit) and test both dependencies directly, rerunning the
simulation chain under reference cosmologies as far as $\sim 10\sigma$
from the baseline --- distance-modulus variations of roughly $0.15$~mag,
including nonstandard $w_0 w_a$ truth models --- and find the effect on
the recovered cosmology subdominant to DES statistical uncertainties.

We compute where the present comparison sits relative to the amplitude
scale of those tests. After covariance-weighted removal of the free
offset, the released-best-fit Model B differs from flat $\Lambda$CDM by
at most $0.055$~mag over the sample ($0.035$~mag against
$\Omega_m = 0.315$), and the pre-BBC-best-fit curve by at most
$0.053$~mag --- substantially below the $\sim 0.15$~mag
reference-cosmology variation Camilleri et al.\ explicitly tested. By
contrast the Einstein--de Sitter control differs by $0.276$~mag, a factor
$1.8$ \emph{above} that tested amplitude, and is precisely the probe
exhibiting two orders of magnitude larger leverage.

This amplitude comparison places the headline probe well within the scale
of the Camilleri tests, and the conclusion accordingly cuts in the
incumbent's favour for the headline comparison: prior DES simulation
tests indicate the reference-cosmology part of the measured leverage is
small at this amplitude. Two caveats define what remains: Camilleri's
formal validation criterion is the shape envelope bounded by the
reference curves of their Fig.~4e, so the present scalar comparison is an
amplitude argument rather than the pointwise shape-containment test
(reconstructing those boundary cosmologies and testing containment
directly is the named upgrade); and the envelope was established with
FLRW-family reference simulations, so its coverage of non-FLRW functional
forms (such as Model B's logarithmic distance plus saturating
attenuation) is inferential rather than direct. The direct completion for both cases is a matched
rerun of the bias-correction simulations with the alternative law as the
reference input, comparing
$L_{\mathrm{cosmology}} =
\Delta\chi^2(\boldsymbol{b}_B) - \Delta\chi^2(\boldsymbol{b}_A)$
against the released margin; \citet{Camilleri2024} provide the iterative
prescription such a test would follow.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the released BBC-related systematic modes}
\label{sec:syssub}

The release provides single-systematic covariance blocks for BBC-related
inputs: the intrinsic-scatter model (BS20), dust/population realizations
(P21SYS1--3, P21\_HOSTCOLOR, W22\_AGE), host library (SVAHOSTLIB),
selection efficiency (HOSTEFFshift), core-collapse likelihood
(CClikelihood), and classifier swaps (SNNtraining, SNIRF, SCONE). None of
these is a reference-cosmology variation, so this test does \emph{not}
isolate the conditioned component; it measures the verdict movement
carried by the released systematic budget these modes represent. Each
block is (near) rank-1, $C_k \approx \boldsymbol{d}_k
\boldsymbol{d}_k^{T}$; perturbing the released moduli by
$\pm\boldsymbol{d}_k$ and refitting both models yields per-input verdict
shifts between $\pm 0.01$ and $\pm 0.40$ $\chi^2$ units, with quadrature
sum $0.71$ against the released margin of $1.1$. The quadrature
sensitivity of the pairwise verdict to these twelve selected modes is
therefore below the margin --- the declared budget they represent does not
by itself overturn the verdict --- while the reference-cosmology question
is addressed by the simulation reruns of \S\ref{sec:envelope}, not by
this decomposition.

\subsection{Paired corrections, and the definitive instrument}

A useful intermediate is a paired-correction experiment: one common,
empirically anchored survey-selection model; corrections computed under
each candidate law; the verdict reported together with its spread across
the two schemes. If the spread is comparable to the claimed
discrimination, corrected-modulus comparison has not produced a
conditioning-independent verdict. The definitive instrument is
likelihood-level forward modelling: each candidate law propagated through
the same population, intrinsic-scatter, cadence, detection,
classification, and cut architecture, compared with the survey at the
likelihood level. Selection is a generative problem; simulation should
not be eliminated but shared.

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

The analysis requires the public v1.2 release and a scientific Python
environment; the reference implementation is at
\href{https://github.com/tracyphasespace/Model-Discrimination-DES-SN5YR}{github.com/tracyphasespace/Model-Discrimination-DES-SN5YR}
(seven scripts; each regenerates the numbers of the section named).

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Freeze the data.} Fetch \texttt{DES-SN5YR\_HD+MetaData.csv}
  and \texttt{STAT+SYS.txt.gz} at tag v1.2 (\texttt{data/fetch.sh});
  the twelve single-systematic blocks via
  \texttt{data/fetch\_systematics.sh}; record hashes.
\item \emph{Load and clean.} Default cut $\texttt{MUERR\_FINAL} \leq 10$
  gives $N = 1768$; the all-1829 variant is one environment flag. Build
  $C = C_{\mathrm{sys}} + \mathrm{diag}(\texttt{MUERR\_FINAL}^2)$
  (positive-definite; $C_{\mathrm{sys}}$ alone is not).
\item \emph{Establish the sign empirically} (\texttt{sign\_check.py};
  \S\ref{sec:sign}). Do not proceed until this passes.
\item \emph{Characterize the layer} without reference to Model B: median,
  extrema, binned medians, regression slope.
\item \emph{Implement both laws} exactly as \S\ref{sec:laws}, including
  the $z_{\mathrm{HD}}$/$z_{\mathrm{HEL}}$ convention; marginalize the
  offset analytically.
\item \emph{Fit and bracket} (\texttt{bracket.py}); confirm
  Table~\ref{tab:headline} and $L_{\mathrm{BBC}} = -11.7$.
\item \emph{Projection and decomposition} (\texttt{projection.py});
  confirm Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rhovals}--\eqref{eq:adapt} including the
  identity check.
\item \emph{Robustness and probes} (\texttt{robustness.py},
  \texttt{probes.py}); confirm Table~\ref{tab:probes}.
\item \emph{Amplitude comparison} (\texttt{envelope.py}); confirm the
  \S\ref{sec:envelope} placements.
\item \emph{Systematic modes} (\texttt{bbc\_systematic\_modes.py});
  confirm the $0.71$ quadrature.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Falsification criterion.} The central claim fails if an independent
implementation with the verified sign convention, frozen v1.2 data, the
official redshift convention, matched covariance ordering, full
covariance, and correct offset marginalization produces a correction-step
swing that is not large relative to the released discrimination. We
invite that check specifically. The first two implementation errors found
by this procedure were the authors' own (a sign-convention error and a
redshift-convention simplification), both corrected before freezing;
that is why the procedure is included.

\section{Discussion}

The result should not be read as an indictment of simulation-based bias
correction. A flux-limited survey requires a model of the objects it
fails to observe, and the pre-BBC endpoint demonstrates the point as
clearly as it demonstrates sensitivity: the goodness of fit deteriorates
substantially when the correction is backed out. Nor should it be read as
a claim that DES has not tested cosmology dependence:
\citet{Camilleri2024} tested the reference-cosmology dependence directly
over a substantial amplitude range and found it small there, and the
headline comparison of this paper sits well within that amplitude scale.

What the measurements here add is the size of the container: the total
BBC step carries roughly eleven times the released margin in pairwise
leverage, dominated by direct covariance alignment rather than parameter
accommodation, with a probe-dependent sign. When the correction step is
that much larger than the verdict, the verdict's meaning rests entirely
on the demonstrated smallness of the conditioned component --- which
existing validation supplies at the tested amplitude scale and not
beyond it, and supplies by simulation rerun for FLRW-family forms rather
than by direct test of non-FLRW functional shapes. The completion is specific:
matched reruns with alternative laws as reference input, paired
corrections against an empirically anchored selection model, and
ultimately likelihood-level forward modelling. The paper asks for those
measurements, not a presumption in either direction.

\section{Conclusion}

The DES-SN5YR v1.2 release carries a simulation-derived BBC correction
with substantial redshift structure. Under the official redshift
convention the released vector gives $\Delta\chi^2 = +1.1$ (Model B
probe), the reconstructed pre-BBC vector $+12.8$; the pairwise
discrimination leverage is $L_{\mathrm{BBC}} = -11.7$, roughly eleven
times the released discrimination, directed toward the probe, and
unchanged when all 1829 entries are retained. Most of the leverage is
direct covariance alignment ($L^{\mathrm{fixed}} = -10.1$) rather than
parameter accommodation ($-1.6$). Two further probes show the
sensitivity is not unique to the discovering law, with probe-dependent
sign and hence no consistent partisanship. The released-best-fit probe
departs from $\Lambda$CDM by at most $0.055$~mag offset-removed --- well
below the $\sim 0.15$~mag reference-cosmology variation
\citet{Camilleri2024} explicitly tested --- so for this comparison prior
DES simulation tests indicate the conditioned component of the leverage
is small at this amplitude scale; the untested regime begins at larger
departures --- where our Einstein--de Sitter control ($0.276$~mag) shows
two orders of magnitude larger leverage --- and includes the pointwise
shape-containment test and the direct coverage of non-FLRW functional
forms. The
quantitative requirement, Eq.~\eqref{eq:condreq}, and the completion
programme (matched reference-input reruns, paired corrections,
likelihood-level forward modelling) define what remains to be measured
before few-$\Delta\chi^2$ discrimination against distance laws far from
the reference family can be considered conditioning-independent.

\section*{Author's note on scope}

This paper contains no proposed cosmological framework. The law of
\S\ref{sec:laws} is a probe used to test the sensitivity of pairwise model
discrimination to a simulation-derived correction layer. A reader may
reject that law physically without affecting the empirical
characterization of \texttt{biasCor\_mu}, the reconstruction of the
pre-BBC vector, the envelope placement, or the completion programme
defined above. A separate treatment of the propagation physics motivating
the functional form of Model B is logically independent of this analysis.
